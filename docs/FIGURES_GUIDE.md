# Figures Guide — What Each Plot Shows and Why It Matters

Companion to `docs/ROUTING_README.md` (system/methods/rationale) and
`docs/RESEARCH_LOG.md` (chronological log). This document walks through every
figure the project produces: what it shows, how to read it, and the
specific insight it supports — written so figure captions and results-section
prose can be drafted straight from it.

All figures are generated by `plot_results.py` (per-experiment),
`plot_cross_experiment.py` (Mac vs. DGX-Qwen vs. DGX-Llama), and
`analysis/plot_cost_model_results.py` (learned-policy results).
Every figure ships as both `.png` and `.pdf`.

## 1. Per-experiment figures

Generated once per experiment, in `results/figures/` (Mac),
`results/figures/dgx_qwen/`, `results/figures/dgx_llama/` — same six figure
types in each, so any given `figN_*` name means the same thing across all
three directories, just scoped to that experiment's own data.

### `fig1_pareto` — quality vs. energy, by model size × task difficulty

One scatter point per (model tier, task difficulty tier) combination —
9 points total (3 sizes × 3 difficulty levels), connected by a line per
model size. **Reading it**: a line that rises left-to-right within a model
size means harder tasks cost more energy for that model, as expected; a
model-size line that *dips* between difficulty levels (visible on Mac,
medium tasks scoring higher than the "harder" tier for the same model) is
the visual seed of Finding 4/5 — task difficulty isn't a clean scalar.
**Use for**: the paper's first motivating figure ("not every task needs the
largest model").

### `fig2_policy_comparison` — quality and energy by hand-designed policy

Two bar panels (quality %, energy J) with error bars (±1 stdev across the
4 repeats), one bar per policy: `always-largest`, `always-smallest`,
`static-cost-router`, `energy-aware (abundant)`, `energy-aware (scarce)`,
`energy-aware (scarce, calibrated)`. **Note**: this figure predates Option C
and does *not* include the learned policy — see §3 below
(`fig_baseline_pareto_*`, `fig_cross_experiment_summary`) for the figures
that add it in. **Reading it**: `abundant` and `static-cost-router` bars
should be visually identical (they're the same policy by construction — a
built-in sanity check, not a coincidence). **Use for**: showing the
static-vs-scarce quality/energy trade-off per experiment.

### `fig3_metrics_{db_sql,sembench_taken3,sembench_antman}` — quality/energy/latency by model size, per dataset

Three side-by-side panels (quality, energy, latency), bars by model tier,
one figure per dataset within the experiment. **Reading it**: compare the
`sembench_antman` version across experiments — this is where the
model-size *inversion* lives (small beating medium beating large on the
balanced-sentiment movie). The `db_sql` version instead shows the more
conventional "bigger is better, at a cost" pattern. **Use for**: the
concrete numeric grounding behind Finding 4 (task hardness isn't
one-dimensional).

### `fig4_pareto_{dataset}` — quality-vs-energy Pareto, per dataset

Same idea as `fig1_pareto` but one dataset at a time, three points (one per
model size) with the frontier line connecting them. **Reading it**: the
shape of this line *per dataset* is the most direct visual evidence for
Finding 4 — on `sembench_antman` the line slopes *down* as you move to
bigger/more-expensive models, which shouldn't happen if "bigger is always
at least as good" were true. **Use for**: per-dataset frontier shape,
feeding into the cross-experiment comparison (§2).

### `fig5_pareto_comparison` — the three datasets' frontiers side by side

`fig4`'s three individual plots combined into one figure with a shared
y-axis, so the *shapes* can be compared at a glance within one experiment.
**Use for**: a compact single-figure way to show "task type changes the
frontier shape," without needing three separate figure references.

### `fig6_query_heatmap` — every individual task, not aggregated

Two heatmap panels (accuracy, energy) with all 40 individual task IDs on
the y-axis and the 3 model tiers on the x-axis — the fine-grained view
underneath every aggregate number elsewhere. Energy is normalized *per
column* (per model tier), not on one shared scale, because raw energy
magnitudes differ by ~40x between the small and large tiers — a shared
scale would flatten all the small/medium variation to invisible. **Reading
it**: look for outlier rows — e.g. the very first task run against each
model in a sweep shows a visibly higher energy value than the rest of that
column, a cold-model-load effect visible at the individual-task level that
the aggregate figures average away. **Use for**: spot-checking any specific
number that looks surprising in the aggregate figures, and as an appendix
figure demonstrating result density/reproducibility.

## 2. Cross-experiment figures (`results/figures/cross_experiment/`)

Compare Mac vs. DGX-Qwen vs. DGX-Llama directly — the figures that carry
the paper's "does the effect hold as the capability gap widens" argument.

### `fig_cross_pareto` — the three frontiers side by side, log-x

Same idea as `fig5_pareto_comparison`, but across *experiments* instead of
datasets within one experiment: three panels (Mac / DGX-Qwen / DGX-Llama),
small/medium/large frontier in each, log-scaled energy axis so the ~100x
range across experiments is visible in one figure. **Reading it**: the
frontier visibly stretches and steepens left-to-right — Mac's line is
nearly flat (medium ≈ large in quality), DGX-Qwen's rises noticeably from
medium to large, DGX-Llama's rises steeply. This is the single figure that
most directly shows "the capability gap widens as you move Mac → DGX-Qwen →
DGX-Llama," which is the premise the rest of the results depend on.

### `fig_cross_policy` — does the free lunch hold?

Two panels (quality, energy — energy log-scaled), grouped bars for
`static-cost-router` / `scarce` / `scarce, calibrated`, one group per
experiment. **Reading it**: on the quality panel, `scarce`'s bar visibly
collapses on DGX-Qwen and DGX-Llama while `calibrated` stays level with
static; on the energy panel, `calibrated`'s bar matches static exactly on
those same two experiments (no visible gap = 0% saved). **Use for**: the
headline "before Option C" figure — this is exactly what the learned-policy
result (§3) is the answer to.

## 3. Learned cost-model figures (`results/figures/cost_model/`)

Generated by `analysis/plot_cost_model_results.py`, from
`results/baseline_comparison_<experiment>.csv` and
`results/policy_decisions_learned_<experiment>.csv`. These are the figures
that show Option C actually closing the gap `fig_cross_policy` shows.

### `fig_baseline_pareto_{experiment}` — every policy, one scatter

All hand-designed policies, `always-small/medium/large`, the learned policy
(★), and both oracles (gray diamonds) on one quality-vs-energy scatter per
experiment, legend-keyed rather than direct-labeled. **Reading it — this is
the most information-dense single figure in the project**: on DGX-Llama,
`static-cost-router`, `scarce, calibrated`, and the learned policy sit
almost exactly on top of each other (~28,000J, ~87–88%), visually
confirming the learned policy converges to near-static behavior when the
capability gap leaves little safe room — while `scarce` sits far below in
quality at barely more energy than `always-small`, showing exactly how much
quality that policy was giving up for not much energy gained. The two gray
oracle diamonds sit above the cluster, showing the theoretical headroom
that's mostly unreachable at this capability gap. **Use for**: the paper's
main per-experiment routing-outcomes figure — one figure per experiment, or
pick DGX-Llama alone as the most telling single instance.

### `fig_routing_behavior_{experiment}` — what the learned policy actually chose, by workload class

A stacked bar per `task_type_tier` (e.g. `sql_easy`, `sql_hard`,
`semantic_hard`, `concept_hard`, ...) showing the fraction of
(task × repeat) decisions routed to each model tier. **Reading it — this is
the figure that shows the learned policy rediscovering Finding 5 on its
own, without being told to**: on DGX-Qwen, `semantic_hard` routes ~90% to
`large` (barely any downgrading — the policy learned this workload class is
risky to downgrade), while `sql_hard` shows real mixed routing across all
three tiers (the policy learned this class tolerates downgrading much
better). No hand-written rule distinguishes SQL-structural from
semantic-reasoning difficulty anywhere in the router's code — this
distinction emerged from the learned model. **Use for**: strong evidence for
the paper's claim that task "hardness" isn't one-dimensional, now shown as
something the *policy* discovered, not just something the *researchers*
observed in aggregate accuracy tables.

### `fig_cross_experiment_summary` — the headline result, one figure

Two panels (% energy saved, quality diff in pp vs. `static-cost-router`),
grouped bars for `scarce` / `scarce, calibrated` / `learned cost-model
policy`, across all three experiments, with a dotted red line marking the
−2pp quality budget. **Reading it**: on the quality panel, `scarce`'s bars
dive far below the −2pp line on DGX-Qwen/DGX-Llama while both `calibrated`
and the learned policy stay at or above it everywhere; on the energy panel,
`calibrated`'s bar is **entirely absent** on DGX-Qwen and DGX-Llama (exactly
0%) while the learned policy's teal bar is small but clearly present on
both. **This is the single figure that answers §9's question** ("does the
learned policy recover nonzero energy savings where `calibrated` finds
none?") — recommended as the paper's primary results figure for the Option
C section.

## 4. Key insights, by figure

A quick index from claim → supporting figure, for drafting the results
section:

| Claim | Figure(s) |
|---|---|
| Not every task needs the largest model | `fig1_pareto` (any experiment) |
| Task difficulty isn't one-dimensional; model size can even hurt on semantic tasks | `fig3_metrics_sembench_antman`, `fig4_pareto_sembench_antman` |
| The capability gap genuinely widens Mac → DGX-Qwen → DGX-Llama | `fig_cross_pareto` |
| The hand-tuned `scarce` policy's free lunch doesn't survive a widening gap | `fig_cross_policy` |
| A binary calibrated threshold trades all energy savings for safety on DGX | `fig_cross_policy` (energy panel, `calibrated` bars) |
| The learned policy recovers real, nonzero, bounded savings where `calibrated` found none | `fig_cross_experiment_summary`, `fig_baseline_pareto_dgx_qwen`/`_dgx_llama` |
| The learned policy independently discovers the SQL-vs-semantic difficulty distinction | `fig_routing_behavior_{experiment}` |
| On DGX-Llama specifically, the learned policy converges to near-static behavior (little safe room to downgrade) | `fig_baseline_pareto_dgx_llama` (tight point cluster) |
